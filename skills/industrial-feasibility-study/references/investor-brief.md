# Investor meeting brief

Write for the decision at this meeting. Lead with the proposed project, status, decision needed, and the most material condition. Follow with a compact set of sourced figures: capacity and planned sales, total uses and proposed funding, cash need or key return metrics, and price/effective date. Label model outputs and distinguish project and equity metrics. If finance cannot be reproduced, say what figures are reported and what underlying cash-flow evidence is missing.

Order topics by ability to delay the project, increase cash requirements, or reverse the investment case. For each topic, state what the evidence supports, why it matters, a targeted question and the document or decision needed. Typical questions:

- **Sales and price.** What contracted tonnage, delivery terms, price and payment period support the ramp? Request customer commitments or a dated demand basis.
- **Inputs and execution.** Which suppliers, utilities, permits and milestones are confirmed? Request dated quotes, utility commitments, permit records or vendor schedules as appropriate.
- **Funding.** Which lender terms and collateral are approved, when can drawings occur, and how is a funding gap covered? Request the term sheet, sources and uses, and debt schedule.
- **Equity.** What amount and timing does each shareholder commit, what governs distributions and additional capital, and what are the exit terms? Request the agreement or proposed term sheet when relevant.
- **Downside.** What combination of delays, lower sales and higher costs would exhaust liquidity or miss the return threshold? Request the editable workbook and an assumptions log.

Use plain Persian when requested, generally a short paragraph plus a few material topics. Give each question a concrete purpose. End with the documents or decisions required for the next review. A meeting brief is a decision aid. Apply any company minutes template only when the requested document is meeting minutes.
