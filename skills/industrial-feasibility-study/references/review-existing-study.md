# Review an existing feasibility study

1. Identify the author, version, date, currency and scale, valuation basis, construction and operating periods, project boundary, and files available. Check relevant source pages visually if text extraction loses columns, signs, footnotes, digits, or RTL order. Cite printed page/table numbers and distinguish them from PDF page indexes where useful.
2. Extract material values and statuses into the evidence register. Keep source labels, especially when the same heading appears for different years or when a report uses ambiguous terms such as "base year" and "profit." Collect the workbook, quote appendix, or lender terms if available. Note what was inspected.
3. Recompute what the evidence permits using tools. Compare with the report using its disclosed precision and sign convention. Label results **matched**, **discrepancy**, **plausible but unverified**, or **unreproducible from supplied evidence**. Tabulate both values when a difference matters.
4. Assess what changes the investment decision. For each material finding state the source locator, issue, possible effect, and evidence or corrective action required. Distinguish confirmed arithmetic errors from apparent discrepancies, unclear denominators, rounding, method differences, and unsupported assumptions. A missing attachment is a limitation of the evidence available.

| Check | Reconciliation |
|---|---|
| Totals and units | Components to subtotal, fixed investment plus working capital to total, sources to uses, monetary scale and mass units, totals against rounding. |
| Timing | Construction, commissioning and sales ramp, spend dates, lending and repayment dates, quote dates and price base. |
| Production and sales | Available hours, uptime, yield and rate support production. Opening inventory plus production less losses supports sales and ending inventory. Verify each period's volume × net unit price. |
| Commercial evidence | Distinguish forecasts, inquiries, letters of intent, binding commitments, and realized volumes. Check price and delivery basis and buyer concentration. |
| Print versus model | Compare headline, tables, narrative and workbook, including gross versus net amounts, utilization and breakeven denominators, and project versus equity metrics. |
| Cash and debt | Trace accounting profit to cash flow and test whether funds arrive before payment obligations. Reconcile capital, interest, taxes and debt balance when schedules exist. |
| Sensitivity | Match scenario labels and periods, verify baseline and recompute supported scenarios. Flag duplicated results or surprising direction after considering possible nonlinearities and rounding. |
| Financial reproducibility | Verify dated cash flows, valuation base and discount method before validating NPV, IRR or payback. If present-value totals, a benefit/cost ratio or a profitability index are supplied, reconcile each metric only on its stated perspective, scope and valuation basis. Ask for a mapping when definitions or bases are unclear. If inputs are absent, report the headline as sourced and request the workbook. |

When updating, retain source values and conclusions as of the original date. Log the effective date and provenance of each replacement. Identify affected formulas and downstream narrative, then check Version A versus Version B with like-for-like scope. If scope or timing changes, document the effect separately where it can be measured.
